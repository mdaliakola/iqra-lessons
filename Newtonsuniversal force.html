Understanding Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that depends on their masses and the distance between them.

1. Mass Direct Proportionality

Gravitational force is directly proportional to the product of both masses ($F \propto m_1 \cdot m_2$). If you double either mass, the gravitational pull doubles!

m ↑ ⟹ F ↑ (Direct Relationship)

2. Inverse Square Distance Law

Force decreases rapidly with distance — proportional to $1/r^2$. Moving 2x further away makes the attraction 4 times weaker ($2^2 = 4$).

r × 2 ⟹ F ÷ 4 (Inverse-Square)

3. Gravitational Constant (G)

The constant $G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$ represents the fundamental strength of gravity in our universe. Because $G$ is extremely small, gravity is only noticeable with massive objects like planets.

G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²

Newton's Third Law in Gravitation

The force object 1 exerts on object 2 is ALWAYS equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force object 2 exerts on object 1 ($F_{12} = -F_{21}$). An apple pulls Earth with the exact same force Earth pulls the apple!
